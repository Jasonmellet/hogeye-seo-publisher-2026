### Setup facts (HogEye user manual, Sept 2026, and `/camera-resources/` FAQ, Oct 20, 2026)

- Once powered, the LOW, MED, and HIGH lights on the bottom of the camera show signal strength at that spot.
- A paddle antenna comes with the camera. Omni-directional and directional antenna kits are optional.
- Mount the camera about 1 foot from the trap, above it and looking slightly down. Too far from the trap can cause missed notifications.
- The HogEye Mini Camera includes a 50Ah lithium battery (covered by the warranty) and a solar panel with charge controller.
- Face the solar panel south (Northern Hemisphere) with nothing shading it. It needs at least 3 hours of sun to keep the battery charged; the more horizontal the better.
- A fully charged battery with no solar lasts 5 to 6 days on average; with solar it lasts several months with direct sun on the panel.
- A gate cable and latch adapter connect the camera to the trap's gate. The clear end of the gate cable lights when you send a drop request.
- Support: 855-464-3935, support@wildlifedominion.com, or chat in the HogEye app.

## How a HogEye trap closes

- The landowner watches the trap live in the HogEye app, counts the hogs inside and outside, and when the whole sounder is inside sends the close command from the phone. The gate closes.
- The landowner makes that call. The camera does not decide or close on its own.
- Closing on part of a sounder teaches the rest to avoid the trap.

## Hog behavior (agency sources)

- Texas A&M AgriLife Extension (Billy Higginbotham, "Feral Hogs: The Good, The Bad or The Ugly?", agrilifeextension.tamu.edu, March 2026): feral hogs are routinely nocturnal during summer heat; in winter they may become more active during the daytime as they search for food; hunting pressure can result in a hog population rarely seen in daylight, regardless of season.
