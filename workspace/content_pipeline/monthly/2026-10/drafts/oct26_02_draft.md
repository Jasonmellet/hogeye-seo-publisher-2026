# Hog Cams: What a Trap Camera Should Actually Do

**TL;DR:** A hog cam should show the trap live, let you count the full sounder, and leave the closure command with your team. It is a wild hog trap camera system for catch decisions. It is not a general property camera, and it does not close the gate by itself.

A hog cam that only proves something moved last night can help you scout. The catch site needs a harder answer: is the complete sounder inside right now, and can you send the closure command before they leave?

## What a Hog Cam Is

A hog cam, in HogEye terms, is a wild hog trap camera system. It monitors the trap site, shows a 24/7 live video feed for trap decisions, and gives the operator a remote trigger when closure criteria are met. The [HogEye steel camera](https://hogeyecameras.com/steel-camera/) is that tool: cellular, trap-mounted, and paired with a trapping-specific app.

It works with any trap you already run, including commercial corrals, homemade panels, drop traps, and nets. [Big Pig Traps and Boar Blanket](https://hogeyecameras.com/hog-traps/) are separate trap systems if you need the trap itself. The camera job stays the same on each one: see the commitment, then close on purpose.

It is not a general wildlife observer you forget in a tree. It is built for landowners, land managers, and trappers who already run a trap.

## What It Should Show

The view has to support a count. Frame the bait, the entry, and enough of the interior that you can tell animals inside from animals still outside. A wider field of view is the point of the current Mini: see inside and outside the trap in one read.

You should be able to check that view day or night without driving. Digital pan, tilt, and zoom let you inspect the edge when the first frame is not enough. If glare, a panel, or a branch hides the back corner, fix the mount before catch phase. Megapixels will not invent a hog the lens cannot see.

Uptime is part of the picture. The HogEye Mini is about 75% smaller than the legacy camera and uses about 60% less power, with a compact battery and a small solar panel included. Test reporting from the final mount. Bars on the road do not count. Install references are in [camera resources](https://hogeyecameras.com/camera-resources/).

## What It Should Let You Do

Watch. Count. Decide. Trigger.

Remote trap monitoring and closure means your team can act when the window opens, including at 2am, instead of discovering an empty trap on the morning loop. The sequence is simple:

1. Keep the site baited and the camera reporting.
2. Learn the sounder over multiple days before you arm for a catch.
3. Wait until the complete sounder is inside and settled.
4. Send the closure command yourself.

The hog cam does not close a gate, drop a net, or trip an actuator on its own. You send the command. Early closure, with part of the sounder still outside, educates the survivors. [Common hog trap mistakes](https://hogeyecameras.com/hog-trapping-techniques-common-hog-trap-mistakes/) start there.

On a net, the same camera supports a different geometry. Condition with the net high, keep bait available, and switch to catch phase remotely when the full sounder is present. The operator still makes that switch. Details of the net workflow live on the [net camera page](https://hogeyecameras.com/net-camera-trap-remote-hog-trapping/).

## What It Is Not

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should a hog cam actually do?

Show the trap live, support a sounder count inside and outside the trap, and let you send a remote closure command when the full sounder is committed.

### Does the camera close the trap by itself?

No. You watch the feed and send the closure command. The camera does not decide that the sounder is complete.

### Will it work on a homemade trap?

Yes. HogEye works with commercial and homemade traps, including corral, drop, and net setups. Match the mount to the closure line you need to see.

## Match the Camera to the Catch

A hog cam earns its place on the trap when it improves closure timing and cuts empty drives. Put that live read on the primary catch site first. Scout cameras can wait.
