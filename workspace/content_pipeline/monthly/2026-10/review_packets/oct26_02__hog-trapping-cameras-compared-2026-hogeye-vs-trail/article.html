Best Camera for Hog Trapping: Live Video vs. Trail Cameras

If hogs are coming to your trap, the best camera for hog trapping is one that shows you live video and lets you close the gate from your phone. A trail camera can show that hogs were there, but it can’t tell you that the whole sounder is inside right now.

What a trail camera tells you at a trap

A trail camera takes a photo or short clip when something moves. A cellular model sends it to your phone, but the alert can arrive 30 to 90 seconds after the hogs set it off. A sounder can walk in, feed, and leave in a couple of minutes, so by the time you see the alert, the hogs may already be heading out.

A trail camera also can’t close the gate. You still have to drive out and close it yourself, going on a photo that’s already old. Close on part of the sounder and the hogs that get away can learn to avoid the trap.

What a trap camera has to do

You need to see the hogs inside and outside the trap while they’re there. The HogEye Mini Camera streams live video to the HogEye app, day or night. Use digital pan, tilt, and zoom to check the trap, count the hogs inside and outside, and watch as the sounder comes in.

When the whole sounder is inside, send the close command from your phone and the gate closes.

The HogEye Mini Camera works with corral, panel, drop, net, and homemade traps. It comes with a compact battery and small solar panel.

Where a trail camera still helps

A trail camera is useful before you build or move a trap. Use photos to find where hogs are feeding and get an idea of how many are using the area. Look for fresh rooting, trails, wallows, and rubs as you choose a location. Once hogs are coming to the trap, live video gives you the view you need to judge when to close.
