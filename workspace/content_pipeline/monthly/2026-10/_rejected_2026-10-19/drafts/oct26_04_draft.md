# Alabama Hog Trap Monitoring for Trap Lines

**TL;DR:** Alabama hog trap monitoring works when the camera reports from the trap, not from the road. Read the live view before you drive, count the full sounder, and send the closure command only when that group is inside. Season and license rules are a separate state question.

Alabama trap lines run through pine, river bottoms, and pasture edges. Humidity, canopy, and distance stack up against signal and uptime. Alabama hog trap monitoring flips the old loop. Check camera health and the live feed first. Then decide on a watch window, a remote closure command, or a truck.

## Place the Camera for the Decision

You need inside and outside the trap so you can count hogs and see who is still on the edge. On Alabama lines, placement is a signal problem and a count problem at the same time.

1. Frame bait, entry, and the ground where hogs circle before they commit.
2. Give the antenna the clearest path you can without giving up that view.
3. Gain height when a creek bottom or pine canopy kills the report. A higher mount on the same site often beats moving the trap off the sounder.
4. Test from the final mount. Bars on the county road do not count.

## Humidity, Canopy, and Uptime

Alabama sites add weather that dry upland mounts see less often:

- Summer foliage that blocks the tower path
- Low, wet ground where hogs travel and signal is weakest
- Heat and moisture on mounts, seals, batteries, and solar panels
- Night activity that makes a wasted morning drive expensive

Keep panels out of deep shade, recheck mounts after storms, and confirm the unit still reports after multi-day rain.

If the report will not hold, work antenna path, carrier, and height before you abandon a productive bottom. ## Run the Line by Priority

Check sites in this order:

1. Alerts or fresh sounder activity
2. Catch-phase traps close to closure criteria
3. Quiet conditioning sites, for health and bait only
4. Known dead sites, then deprioritize

Write the rules down so the whole team sorts the same way: ignore, watch, act remotely, or drive. Map order feels organized and still burns the day on empty traps.

Fresh rooting, trails, wallows, and rubs still mark the corridors, often where coverage is worst. Monitor from the trap. Close at 2am when the window opens, not the next morning after the sounder has left.

## Count the Sounder Before You Close

Bottoms can show hogs early and often. That is not permission to close early. On the live view, answer three questions:

- Are hogs feeding inside, or only testing the edge?
- Does tonight's count match the sounder you have logged over several nights?
- Is anything you expected still outside the trap?

Send the closure command when the whole sounder is inside and settled. The camera does not close the gate by itself. Partial captures educate survivors. Review [common hog trap mistakes](https://hogeyecameras.com/hog-trapping-techniques-common-hog-trap-mistakes/) before you treat the first animals in frame as a finished count.

Season dates, license requirements, and legal methods are set by the Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources, and those rules change. Check the current rules before you trap. The routine above covers uptime and closure timing, not regulations.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Will a cellular camera work on an Alabama trap line?

It can, if you test from the mount, open the antenna path, and add height when canopy blocks the tower. A road check is not a site check.

### Should I move the trap when the bottom has weak signal?

Try antenna path, carrier, and mount height first if the sounder is using the site. Move only when a short shift keeps hogs on bait and the camera starts reporting.

### When should I close on an Alabama sounder?

When the full sounder is inside and settled, based on several nights of counts. You send the closure command. Early activity in a bottom is common. Early closure still educates the rest of the group.
