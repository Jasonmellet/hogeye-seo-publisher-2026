# Barn Owl Hog Trap Camera (RangeCam) vs HogEye

**TL;DR:** Barn Owl's RangeCam Live and HogEye both offer live video. For a hog trap, judge each one by the closure step. HogEye is built around a one-touch remote trigger that closes the trap gate from your phone when the full sounder is inside. Do not buy from unsourced spec sheets.

Landowners often meet Barn Owl and its RangeCam cameras when they want cellular eyes on a remote place. A hog trap asks a tighter question than "did something visit." You need to know whether the complete sounder is committed, then send the closure command before the group splits.

This comparison is about that job. It does not give RangeCam models a price, a detection range, a delay time, or a night-performance score. Check those figures on the manufacturer's current pages before you treat them as facts.

## Start With the Trap-Line Job

Putting a Barn Owl hog trap camera on the shortlist usually means one of two things. You want a camera on a remote trap, or you already own a general observation camera and wonder if it can run catch phase.

The mistake is treating every cellular camera as a trap monitor. Trapping is a decision. General observation answers whether activity happened. Whole-sounder removal asks whether every hog you have been counting is inside right now.

If a unit cannot give you that live count, do not make it the only camera on the gate you plan to close tonight.

## Where Barn Owl's RangeCam Fits

Barn Owl markets its RangeCam cameras for remote viewing over cellular, and its site advertises live video on RangeCam Live. That is a real feature at a trap site: you can watch hogs on the bait instead of waiting on a photo. Features and price vary by model, so check the manufacturer's current specs before you compare.

Live video answers the count question. The trap still needs a closure step. Before you buy any camera for catch phase, ask how you will close the gate or trap when the full sounder is inside, and confirm that answer with the manufacturer.

## A Split That Keeps the Line Honest

Some teams run both kinds of camera:

- A remote-viewing camera on a distant crossing, for activity
- HogEye on the primary catch site, for the live count and the closure command

Do not put catch phase on the slower loop. Standardize the catch-site mount so night review shows the gate line and the back of the trap. [Camera resources](https://hogeyecameras.com/camera-resources/) cover install checks. Confirm the report from the mount, not from the road, before you arm the gate.

Judge the purchase by outcomes: full-sounder captures, fewer empty drives, and closure when the group is inside. A cleaner snapshot of the bait pile is a different product.

### Can I use a Barn Owl camera on a hog trap?
