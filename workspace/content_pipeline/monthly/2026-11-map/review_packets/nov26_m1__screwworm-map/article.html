Screwworm Map for Hog Trappers

If you trap or shoot hogs where screwworm has turned up, check every animal for open, wormy wounds, take a photo, and call the number on the screwworm map for your state that same day. Check the map before you head out so you know which areas have confirmed cases and which reporting contact to use.

What the screwworm map shows

The map shows the Texas Animal Health Commission’s infested and surveillance zones. It also marks each county in Texas and New Mexico with a confirmed screwworm case in 2026 and identifies which cases are still active. If you're new to it, start with what New World screwworm is.

As of October 26, 2026, 23 counties had a confirmed case this year: 18 in Texas and 5 in New Mexico. TAHC lists 18 Texas counties with confirmed detections. A county with a confirmed case is not necessarily still active, so check the map’s status rather than assuming every past detection is ongoing.

The first U.S. animal case of the 2026 outbreak was a calf in Zavala County, announced June 3. That calf recovered, and its case is no longer active.

Why the Pecos County detection matters

On October 21, Texas Parks and Wildlife confirmed screwworm in a hunter-harvested elk in Pecos County. USDA APHIS called it the first detection in U.S. wildlife. After that detection, TAHC said it would change the infested zone, and USDA and Texas Parks and Wildlife are adding wildlife surveillance.

That makes the map useful even if you’re checking a feral hog catch rather than livestock. USDA APHIS reported 1,947 active animal cases in Mexico as of October 19, 2026, and asks people to report anything suspicious right away. Reports help officials see where screwworm is and where to act.

Inspect. Document. Report.

Before field dressing or moving a hog, inspect it closely. Check the eyes, ears, nose, mouth, genitals and between the toes, as well as any open wounds. Screwworm maggots feed on living flesh, so they can be packed deep in a wound rather than sitting only on the surface. For more detail on what to look for, read how to spot New World screwworm.

If you find maggots in a wound, take clear photos and write down where and when you took the animal. Then call the appropriate number the same day. Use the map to confirm the contact for your state, especially if you’re outside Texas or New Mexico. Keep these numbers with your Texas hog monitoring checklist.

  • Texas wildlife, including feral hogs, deer and elk: Texas Parks and Wildlife at 512-389-4505, or your local TPWD wildlife biologist.
  • Texas livestock and pets: TAHC at 1-800-550-8242.
  • New Mexico: New Mexico Livestock Board at 505-841-6161.
  • Wildlife outside Texas: USDA at 866-487-3297 or screwworm@usda.gov. The map lists each focus state’s state veterinarian line.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often is the screwworm map updated?

The Texas zones refresh every 6 hours from TAHC's public map. The case counties are checked by hand each week against TAHC, New Mexico and USDA APHIS releases.

Can I put this map on my own website?

Yes. Other websites can show the map by copying its embed code, and its citation lists the sources and the date it was last updated.

Can a camera tell me if a hog has screwworm?

No. Screwworm maggots sit deep in a wound, so you only find them by looking at each animal up close. A HogEye camera tells you when you have a catch to go look at.
