# HogEye vs Vosker vs Reolink Go: Choosing a Camera for Hog Trapping

If you have shopped for a camera to watch a hog trap, you have probably found the same thing everyone else does: most cellular cameras are built to watch a spot of ground, not to help you catch a sounder. Vosker and Reolink Go are capable, popular cameras. They were just designed for general observation, not for the specific job of trapping feral hogs.

This is a buyer's framework, not a spec sheet. Rather than argue over numbers that change with every model year, it lays out what actually matters when the job is full-sounder removal, and then shows where a purpose-built setup and a general-purpose camera pull in different directions. Use it to decide what you are really buying for.

## Start With the Job, Not the Camera

The mistake is shopping for a camera before defining the task. Watching a feeder, a gate, or a driveway is an observation job: you want to see what shows up. Trapping a sounder is a decision job: you need to know when the whole group is committed so you can trip the gate at the right moment and not before.

Those are different problems. An observation camera answers "what is here?" A trap monitoring system has to answer "is it time?" Once you frame the purchase around the decision instead of the picture, the comparison gets a lot clearer.

## The Criteria That Actually Matter for Hog Trapping

When the goal is catching whole sounders, a short list of capabilities does most of the work:

- **Trap-aware monitoring.** Can the system support the trapping decision (reading sounder activity and committing the gate), or does it only send you images?
- **Durability at the trap site.** Trap sites are rough: livestock, weather, and abuse. Hardware needs to survive being bolted to a panel in the brush, not a quiet tree line.
- **Reliable reporting in weak signal.** Trap sites sit in bottoms and heavy cover where coverage is thin. The unit has to report from where the trap actually is.
- **Support that knows hogs.** When something goes wrong in the field, you want help from people who understand trapping, not a general electronics queue.
- **Fit with your trap and workflow.** The camera should work with how you trap, including the gate or trigger you run.

Judge any camera, including ours, against that list. The right answer is whatever helps you catch the whole sounder with the fewest wasted trips.

## How the Three Compare

### General cellular observation cameras (Vosker)
Vosker makes solar and cellular cameras aimed at general property observation. They are well suited to watching a location and sending images, and many landowners already own one. For trapping, the gap is purpose: an observation camera shows you hogs are present, but it is not built around the trapping decision or the trap-site abuse that comes with it. It can be a useful set of eyes, but it leaves the catch decision entirely on you.

### General cellular cameras (Reolink Go)
Reolink Go is a flexible, general-purpose cellular camera with a broad following. Like other general cameras, its strengths are versatility and price-to-feature value across many uses. The same breadth is the limitation for trapping: it is a camera for everything, which means it is not specialized for the one job of helping you read a sounder and commit a gate, and it is not built specifically for the conditions a hog trap puts hardware through.
