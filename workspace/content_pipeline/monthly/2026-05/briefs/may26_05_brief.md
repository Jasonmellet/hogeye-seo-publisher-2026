## Outline

**H1:** Corral Trap vs. Box Trap vs. Drop Net: Which Hog Trap Is Right for Your Property?

**H2:** The Three Main Wild Hog Trap Categories  
- Brief overview of each: corral (panel trap), box trap, drop/suspended net  
- Who each type is designed for (sounder size, property size, budget)  
- What they all have in common: they require conditioning and proper timing

**H2:** Corral Traps (Panel Traps): Best for Catching the Whole Sounder  
- How corral traps work: large circular or square enclosure with a trip-wire or remote gate  
- (PAA: "What is a corral trap?")  
- Sounder capacity: 10–50+ hogs depending on panel count  
- Cost range: $500–$3,000+ depending on size and gate mechanism  
- Setup requirements: open site, time to condition, transport of panels  
- Trigger options: mechanical trip wire, gravity gate, cellular remote release  
- Best for: landowners with large sounders, multiple trap sites, long-term programs  
- Limitation: requires knowing the sounder is fully inside before closing — nearly impossible without a camera

**H2:** Box Traps: Best for Solo Hogs and Small Groups  
- How box traps work: enclosed metal box with a drop gate or spring door  
- Sounder capacity: 1–4 hogs typically  
- Cost range: $150–$600  
- Setup requirements: minimal — place, bait, wait  
- Best for: small family groups, nuisance single hogs, supplementary trapping alongside a corral program  
- Limitation: won't catch a sounder of 12+ in a single event — requires multiple units run simultaneously

**H2:** Drop Nets and Suspended Net Traps: Best for Large Sounders in Open Areas  
- How net traps work: net suspended above bait, remotely triggered when sounder is underneath  
- Sounder capacity: potentially very large (20–50+) depending on net size  
- Cost range: $2,000–$6,000+ for complete systems  
- Setup requirements: open, flat ground; significant conditioning time; specialized equipment  
- Best for: large open fields, intensive eradication programs, government/agency use  
- Limitation: hogs must be well-conditioned; net must drop cleanly; requires trained handling crew  
- Note: products like Pig Brig and BoarBuster operate in this category

**H2:** Comparison Table

**H2:** How to Choose Based on Your Property and Herd Size  
- Decision tree: small nuisance hog → box trap; sounder of 6–20 → corral; large open property + big sounder → net  
- Budget vs. catch-rate tradeoffs  
- Running multiple trap types simultaneously

**H2:** What All Three Trap Types Have in Common: The Monitoring Requirement  
- You need to know when the sounder is inside — regardless of trap type  
- Corral and net traps make this most critical (you can't close manually at 2am)  
- Box traps benefit from monitoring too — knowing when a catch happens vs. finding an angry hog 12 hours later
