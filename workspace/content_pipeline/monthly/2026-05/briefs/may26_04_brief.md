## Outline

**H1:** Common Hog Trap Mistakes (and How Remote Monitoring Fixes Most of Them)

**H2:** Mistake 1: Placing the Trap Before Conditioning Is Complete  
- Hogs associate the trap hardware with danger before they associate it with food  
- Signs you put the trap in too early (camera evidence)  
- How long to pre-bait before introducing the trap: the 7–14 day rule  
- Remote monitoring role: camera at the bait site during pre-bait phase confirms sounder confidence before trap goes in

**H2:** Mistake 2: Checking the Trap on a Fixed Schedule (Instead of When It Matters)  
- Hogs detect human scent and presence — even after a catch  
- Checking an empty trap at dawn vs. checking a triggered trap at 2am  
- The cost of a missed 2am closure: the sounder exits before you arrive  
- (PAA: "How often should I check my hog trap?")  
- Remote monitoring role: real-time alert + remote closure = no wasted trips, no missed windows

**H2:** Mistake 3: Closing the Trap on the First Hog Instead of the Whole Sounder  
- Catching 2 out of 12 hogs doesn't solve the problem — it makes the rest trap-shy  
- How to identify when the whole sounder is inside (or close to it)  
- The sow hierarchy: if you don't catch the matriarch, the sounder reorganizes  
- Remote monitoring role: live camera view or motion-triggered footage shows sounder count before closing

**H2:** Mistake 4: Using the Wrong Trigger Mechanism for Your Trap Type  
- Drop gates, swing gates, net traps — different triggers have different false-positive rates  
- Wind, debris, and small animals triggering the gate prematurely  
- How to calibrate your trigger  
- Remote monitoring role: camera confirms whether a trigger event caught hogs or was a false positive

**H2:** Mistake 5: Poor Trap Placement (Ignoring Sign and Travel Corridors)  
- Hogs have preferred routes — trap across the corridor, not in a clearing  
- Water proximity in summer  
- Avoiding flood zones, soft ground, and sites with escape routes nearby  
- (PAA: "What is the best location to place hog traps?")

**H2:** Mistake 6: Not Refreshing Bait at the Right Intervals  
- Too frequent: human scent contamination, disrupts conditioning  
- Too infrequent: hogs find nothing and move on  
- The "refresh without disturbing" technique  
- Remote monitoring role: camera shows bait depletion without requiring a site visit

**H2:** Mistake 7: Running a Single Trap When the Problem Requires Multiple  
- A sounder of 15+ hogs can't be caught in one box trap  
- Corral and net traps for large sounders; multiple box traps for dispersed groups  
- Coordination problem: how do you monitor multiple traps without living at the property?  
- Remote monitoring role: multi-camera / multi-trap overview without driving between sites
