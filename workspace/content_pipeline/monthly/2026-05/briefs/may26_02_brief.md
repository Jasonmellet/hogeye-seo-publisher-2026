## Outline

**H1:** Wild Hog Behavior: What Time They Move, How They Scout, and Why It Affects Your Trap

**H2:** Are Wild Hogs Nocturnal? (Answers the #1 PAA question)  
- Primarily nocturnal in high-pressure areas, but adaptable  
- Season and temperature drive activity windows  
- Why "check the trap at dawn" is often wrong

**H2:** How Wild Hogs Scout a New Area  
- Hogs approach bait sites cautiously over multiple nights before committing  
- The "sow leads" pattern — mature sow scouts before the sounder enters  
- What trail camera footage actually shows in the 72 hours before a catch

**H2:** Understanding the Sounder: Structure, Hierarchy, and Why It Matters for Trapping  
- What is a sounder? (PAA: "Why are pigs called sounders?", "How many pigs make a sounder?")  
- Sow + juvenile structure vs. lone boars  
- Why catching the whole sounder requires waiting for the right moment — and knowing when that moment is

**H2:** Seasonal Behavior Changes  
- Spring: breeding activity, boar movement increases  
- Summer: nocturnal concentration around water  
- Fall: acorn mast drives range shifts  
- Winter: more daylight activity in cold climates  
- How to adjust trap timing by season

**H2:** Reading Sign: How to Know Hogs Are in Your Area  
- Rooting patterns (fresh vs. old)  
- Wallow locations  
- Tracks and trail corridors  
- Rubs on fence posts and trees  
- (PAA: "What are some signs of wild hog presence?")

**H2:** How Behavior Should Drive Your Trap Strategy  
- Bait conditioning window: 5–10 nights typical before full sounder commitment  
- Why a fixed check schedule fails: hogs don't read calendars  
- The case for real-time monitoring: close the trap when the sounder is in, not when it's convenient
