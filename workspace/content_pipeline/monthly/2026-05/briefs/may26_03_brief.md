## Angle

Lead with the two things people always get wrong: using the wrong bait for the conditions, and not waiting long enough during the conditioning phase. The conditioning window is where remote monitoring pays off — you don't want to walk in and disturb the site while hogs are still scouting. A camera tells you when to stop refreshing bait and start watching for the right closure moment.

**H2:** Bait Placement Inside the Trap  
- Where to place bait relative to the trigger  
- Quantity: enough to work but not so much that hogs feed without triggering  
- Refreshing bait: how often, and whether to touch the trap at all  
- Scent control: does it matter for hogs? (Mostly no — but here's why)

**H2:** The Conditioning Timeline: What to Expect Night by Night  
- Night 1–2: scout visit (sow, possibly alone)  
- Night 3–5: partial sounder entry, feeding at perimeter  
- Night 6–10: full sounder confidence, trigger zone entry likely  
- When to close: the window between "sounder inside" and "sounder suspicious"  
- (PAA: "How long does it take hogs to find bait?")

**H2:** How a Trap Camera Changes the Baiting Game  
- You can see conditioning progress without visiting the site  
- Real-time alerts when the sounder commits to the bait  
- Closing the trap remotely when the whole group is inside — not hours later  
- The cost of a missed window: displaced sounder, days of re-conditioning

**H2:** Seasonal Bait Adjustments  
- Summer: fermented corn degrades faster in heat — refresh schedule matters  
- Winter: caloric density matters more; add corn + protein attractants  
- Acorn mast season: bait competition is high — use stronger scent attractants

**H2:** What Doesn't Work (and Why Trappers Keep Trying It Anyway)  
- Salt blocks: attract but don't concentrate hogs at trigger  
- Peanut butter: inconsistent, expensive at scale  
- Diesel-corn: hazardous, unreliable, not recommended  
- Poison baiting (sodium nitrite programs): regulated, not a DIY option
