## Who You're Writing For

**The audience is an operational decision-maker with a hog problem:**
- Landowners managing hog pressure across a working property
- Semi-professional or professional trappers running multi-trap programs
- Land managers and ranch foremen responsible for hog damage outcomes
- Farmers dealing with active crop or pasture destruction

**What they care about:**
- Full-sounder capture rate (not individual catches)
- Fewer wasted trips to an empty trap
- Closing when the full sounder is inside — not guessing from the truck
- Reliability. If the system fails at 2am, they lose the window.

**What they do not care about:**
- Specs for their own sake
- Long feature lists
- Being sold to before their problem is acknowledged

**Write to someone who is already in the field.** They know what a sounder is. They know what a corral trap looks like. Respect their time and their intelligence.

## The Voice

**One-sentence summary:** A field-tested operations consultant who knows the work, respects the reader, and measures everything in outcomes.

### Tone markers

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Direct, practical, specific | Vague, theoretical, generic |
| Honest about tradeoffs | Overpromising |
| Operator-focused ("your team," "your trap site") | Customer-focused ("users," "customers") |
| Outcome-first ("reduce missed closure windows") | Feature-first ("HD video with night vision") |
| Confident, not arrogant | Hype, superlatives ("the best," "revolutionary") |

### Sentence structure

**Short sentences for key decisions:**
> "If one link is weak, closure timing quality drops."

**Medium sentences for explanation (15–20 words):**
> "If your team is still trying to run hog trapping with generic camera logic, you are likely losing opportunities in the final step that matters most: the trigger moment."

**Lists for process steps. Always numbered when sequential:**
> 1) Monitor for sounder presence and entry
> 2) Verify full commitment before the trigger decision
> 3) Close remotely when criteria are met — not on impulse

### Tested opening patterns

These patterns appear in approved content and work for this audience:

- **"If your [audience] is still [wrong behavior], you are [consequence]."**  
  *"If your team is still checking traps on a fixed schedule, you are missing closure windows that happen at 2am."*

- **"Most [failures] come from [root cause], not [assumed cause]."**  
  *"Most missed captures come from decision timing errors, not the wrong trap type."*

- **"The strongest teams run [X] like [process model]."**  
  *"The strongest teams run sounder conditioning like a repeatable workflow, not a hope."*

## Concrete field language (May 2026 editorial standard)

Operators should picture the property when they read a sentence. If they cannot, the wording is too vague.

### Prefer evidence over shorthand

| Avoid | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| active sign / real sign / stale sign | **fresh rooting and trails** (add **wallows** or **rubs** when relevant) |
| active zone / equip the first active zone | **sounder zone** or **trap site with fresh rooting and a remote camera** |
| Walk sign / sign pops / sign refreshes | **walk the property**, **fresh damage shows clearly**, **check whether hogs returned** |
| act at the right moment | **close when the full sounder is inside** |
| gear / Buy Gear | **trap panels**, **camera**, **actuator** (name what you mean) |
| trap infrastructure / scaling sites | **mapped trap zones with cameras and logs**, **multiple trap sites on one property** |
| pretty clearing hogs never use | **open clearing with no fresh rooting or trails** |

### Client-facing copy: no internal labels

Google Doc and WordPress drafts must **not** reference batch structure:

- ❌ `Article 1`, `Article 3 in this batch`, `May posts`, `published in May 2026`
- ✅ Linked guide titles: [Texas trapping overview](https://hogeyecameras.com/trapping-wild-hogs-texas/), [electronic hog trap guide](https://hogeyecameras.com/electronic-hog-traps-explained/), etc.

### Allowed exception

In placement-focused content, **reading hog sign** is acceptable as a heading when the section immediately defines **rooting, wallows, trails, and rubs**.

## Hard Rules: The North Star Test

**Every section must answer at least one of these four questions:**

1. How does this help remote hog trap monitoring?
2. How does this improve trap closure timing?
3. How does this improve full-sounder capture outcomes?
4. How does this reduce wasted trips or missed captures?

If a paragraph doesn't answer any of these — cut it or reframe it.

## Hard Rules: What HogEye Is NOT

This is the most common cause of client revision requests. If any of the following language appears in a draft, it will be flagged.

**HogEye is not:**
- A security camera
- An off-grid property camera
- A driveway or gate access camera
- A ranch surveillance system
- A wildlife or hunting camera
- A general-purpose trail camera

**Blacklisted terms — never use:**

> security camera, driveway camera, property security, perimeter security, surveillance, theft, burglary, deliveries, gate access, off-grid security, ranch camera (as a standalone concept), hunting camera, game camera (in a deer/wildlife context), trail camera (used generically)

**If you need to contrast HogEye against generic alternatives, name the category clearly:**
> ✅ "Unlike standard trail cameras designed for wildlife observation..."  
> ❌ "Unlike other trail cameras..."

## Article Structure

Every HogEye blog post follows this shape. Do not deviate without a specific reason documented in the brief.

```
H1: [Primary keyword + outcome framing]

Intro (2–3 paragraphs):
  - Open with a problem statement (pattern above)
  - Frame the reader's current situation
  - State the article's purpose/promise

H2: [Problem/context section]
  - 2–3 paragraphs establishing why this matters
  - Use field-specific language and real scenarios

H2: [Process or knowledge section(s)]
  - The core of the article — what the reader needs to know
  - Use numbered steps for sequences
  - Use bullet lists for options or failure modes
  - Sub-headings (H3) only when a section has 3+ distinct parts

H2: [HogEye connection — earned, not forced]
  - Introduce the monitoring/closure angle naturally
  - Connect to outcomes, not features
  - This should feel inevitable given what was explained above

FAQ (H2: "Frequently Asked Questions"):
  - 4–6 questions using schema format
  - Answer in 1–2 sentences — direct, no filler
  - Include the "Is this a security camera?" objection handler if relevant

CTA (final section, not a H2):
  - 1 bridge sentence ("Ready to [outcome]?")
  - 4–5 internal links to core product/resource pages
  - Soft ask: "Talk with the HogEye team about..."
```

**Word count:** 1,400–2,000 words. Longer only if the topic genuinely requires it.

## FAQ Format (for schema markup)

```
### Is HogEye a security camera?
No. HogEye is designed specifically for wild hog trap operations — monitoring trap sites, verifying sounder commitment, and triggering remote trap closure. It is not a property security product.

### How often should I check my hog trap?
With a remote monitoring system, you don't need a fixed check schedule. The camera alerts you when activity happens — so you respond when it matters, not on a routine that may miss the window.
```

Keep FAQ answers to 1–2 sentences. Do not pad.

## Transition Phrases That Work in This Voice

These appear in approved content and maintain the tone:

- "That distinction matters." *(signals a key contrast)*
- "This is where [X] becomes [outcome]." *(connects feature to result)*
- "Common mistake: [X] / Better approach: [Y]" *(teaches by contrast)*
- "The result is [specific outcome]." *(closes a process section)*
- "Most teams treat [X] as [wrong frame]. It is [right frame]." *(reframes)*

## Evidence and Credibility Signals

Use specific numbers when available. Vague claims lose credibility with this audience.

✅ "If a trapper drives 45 minutes each way and checks 3 traps per week, remote monitoring eliminates most of those empty-trap trips."  
❌ "Remote monitoring saves significant time and money."

✅ "A conservative estimate puts U.S. wild pig costs at a minimum of $3.4 billion a year."  
❌ "Feral hogs cause enormous damage to agriculture."  
❌ Bare national "$2.5B" / "$1.5B" without a clearly scoped older cite (see Schell feedback / brand baseline).

If a specific number isn't available, use relative framing:
> "Most teams find the conditioning window runs 7–14 days for a naive sounder — longer in high-pressure areas."

**Opening sentence:** Problem-first, operator-framing, specific consequence
